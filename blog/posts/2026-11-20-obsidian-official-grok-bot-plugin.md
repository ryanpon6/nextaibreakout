Obsidian shipped an official plugin that lets Grok Bot create, search and manage notes in your Obsidian vault.

That gives a bot somewhere to keep what it learns, in files you own, instead of starting fresh each chat.

## What Obsidian said

> Grok Bot can now connect to your Obsidian vault. ~ [Obsidian (@obsdmd) on X, Nov 19, 2026](https://x.com/obsdmd/status/2123484964268413341)

Per the same post, it can update links, capture content from the web, and create Bases and Canvas files, with changes syncing to your devices through Obsidian Sync.

One user checked Obsidian's help page and quoted it:

> Obsidian is an official plugin for ChatGPT, Codex, Cursor, and Grok Bot. ~ [Eric Starr | Founder, SoundMinds (@EricSoundMinds) on X, Nov 19, 2026](https://x.com/EricSoundMinds/status/2123575858682331207)

## The fair question

Not everyone sees the point yet. One reply asked why a connector is needed at all:

> can it not read the vault folder and update it directly? ~ [Karthik (@kartikktwt) on X, Nov 19, 2026](https://x.com/kartikktwt/status/2123495335754674324)

My read: a bot on its own computer can't see the vault on yours. An official plugin is the supported way in, and it knows Obsidian's own link and file formats.

## Why it matters to me

My bots already share notes and rules through plain files. A vault I can open and read myself is a better home for that than memory I can't see. If you try it, start with one folder and let the bot write new notes before it edits anything you care about.

## What I haven't checked

I haven't installed the plugin yet. I haven't tested how it handles big vaults, what it can delete, or whether it works without an Obsidian Sync subscription.
